Global Era Progression Design

A shared five-era world progression driven by the average development of living civilizations, a passive time safety valve, branching historical outcomes, and catch-up technology costs.

Status: design proposal | Scope: world state, progression, technology availability, UI, save/load, and AI | Exact era conditions remain balance data to be defined later.

1. Short Version

Design statement: the world advances through five shared eras. For every era condition, the game calculates the average value across civilizations that are still alive. A civilization destroyed before the check is immediately removed from both the sum and the divisor. Active development can accelerate the transition, but every era also has a time limit so a damaged civilization cannot freeze the whole match.

The era is global, but the route through history is not fixed. Some transitions have two competing branches, such as an agrarian branch and a military branch. The branch whose condition is resolved first becomes the history of this match. Technologies belonging to the branch that was not selected are unavailable for the rest of the match.

Basic unit and building levels remain shared progression content. Branch-specific technologies are a separate system: they change functionality, production, economy, or strategic identity rather than replacing the ordinary level ladder.

GLOBAL

One world era

All living players enter the next era at the same time. A player cannot privately advance to a separate world era.

AVERAGE

Living civilizations only

Each condition uses the mean of the civilizations currently alive. Elimination changes the divisor immediately.

CATCH-UP

Old research becomes cheaper

Older available technologies cost and research faster, but never below 30% of their base values.

CHOICE

History locks the omitted branch

A branch not selected in this match is not a late-game checklist. Its technologies remain unavailable.

3. Five-Era Structure

The initial structure uses two branch points and one convergence point. It creates four broad historical combinations without requiring a full tree of unrelated content.

The branch changes the world-wide technology set. It does not create a separate private era for each player.

TransitionStructureExample direction
Era 1 to Era 2First branch point; one of two conditions resolves first.Agrarian development versus military mobilization.
Era 2 to Era 3Convergence into a common era.Both histories gain access to the shared consolidation layer.
Era 3 to Era 4Second branch point; a new pair of conditions competes.Science/industry versus expansion/siege.
Era 4 to Era 5Convergence into the final apex era.The selected history determines which earlier technologies were available.

4. Living-Civilization Average

The central progression rule is an average, not a fixed global total. Each condition has its own metric and target. The denominator contains only civilizations that are currently alive.

condition average = sum(condition value for living civilizations) / number of living civilizations

The conditions themselves are deliberately not fixed in this document. A future data definition may use workers, completed military buildings, army value, resource production, territory, or another measurable state. The averaging rule stays the same.

Example A: three living civilizations

Condition: 20 workers per living civilization
          
A player above the target can compensate for another player below it, because the rule evaluates the living world average.

Example B: one civilization is destroyed

Condition: 20 workers per living civilization
          
Player C is removed immediately from the sum and divisor. Elimination can therefore accelerate the era transition as well as weaken the defeated side.
Intentional snowball interaction: the living-only denominator makes elimination a progression event. This is a deliberate consequence of the rule, not a hidden exception. Balance data should account for the possibility that a winning player can both remove an opponent and bring the world closer to the next era.

4.1 What counts as alive?

A civilization is included until the game recognizes it as destroyed according to the normal defeat rule. The transition should happen at a deterministic simulation point, not during rendering. Once the defeat state is committed, later condition calculations in that tick use the updated living-player set.

The implementation should not infer life from a single unit or building count unless that is already the game's authoritative defeat rule. The world progression system should consume a clear player status such as active, defeated, or an equivalent runtime state.

4.2 Multiple conditions

Conditions are evaluated independently. A transition may require several averages to be satisfied at the same time, or a branch may have alternative condition sets. The exact combination is future balance data.

5. Active Progress and the Time Safety Valve

Era progress has two cooperating sources. The active source reflects what civilizations are doing. The passive source is a bounded automatic transition that prevents a damaged match from becoming permanently stuck.

Both sources feed one deterministic controller. The timer is a fallback, not a second player-controlled progression track.

Progress sourceWhat it representsWhy it exists
Active contributionCurrent condition averages and branch-specific thresholds.Rewards development, military pressure, and deliberate world shaping.
Passive timerElapsed fixed-step simulation time since the current era began.Guarantees eventual movement when war or economic collapse prevents normal development.

All timing must use fixed simulation ticks. The current simulation runs at approximately 30 updates per second, so era timers should be represented in simulation time rather than renderer frames.

6. Branching Historical Outcomes

A branch point presents two or more possible histories. Each candidate has its own condition set and progress. The first candidate to resolve wins the branch for the match.

Era 2A: Agrarian path

Example only This path could require a high living average of workers and farms before the military condition resolves.

  • Unlocks farming, logistics, trade, and defensive technologies.
  • Encourages a player to grow the economy and protect infrastructure.
  • Can be accelerated by producing the required economic assets across living civilizations.

Era 2B: Military path

Example only This path could require a high living average of army value and completed military buildings.

  • Unlocks mobilization, forging, recruitment, and offensive technologies.
  • Encourages visible military investment and map pressure.
  • Can be accelerated by building and maintaining a meaningful army.

Once a branch resolves, the losing branch is not converted into a late-game shopping list. Its technologies are unavailable in that match. This makes the global race consequential: players are competing not only over when the world advances, but also over which content the world will receive.

Branch scope: the lock is match-local. A new match can select the other history. The rule does not permanently delete the omitted technology from the game's data or from future matches.

6.1 Timeout behavior

If no branch has resolved when the era timer expires, the transition needs a deterministic fallback. The recommended first rule is to select the candidate with the greater normalized progress at timeout. If the candidates are tied, use a neutral fallback or a stable map-defined tie-breaker.

7. Technology Availability and Depreciation

Technology has two independent dimensions: whether it is available in this match, and how expensive it is at the current world era.

Technology categoryAvailabilityPurpose
Shared entity levelsAvailable through the normal unit/building level ladder.Common improvements such as attack, armor, HP, production, or other level-defined statistics.
Selected branch technologyAvailable when its era and prerequisites are reached.Defines the chosen global history through functional and strategic effects.
Omitted branch technologyUnavailable for the rest of the current match.Preserves the consequence of the global historical choice.
Older available technologyStill available if it belongs to the selected history and prerequisites are met.Enables catch-up without reopening the omitted branch.

7.1 Depreciation schedule

When a technology becomes older than the current world era, its base resource cost and research time are multiplied by the schedule below. The lower the multiplier, the stronger the catch-up. The multiplier never falls below 30%.

Era differenceResource costResearch timeMeaning
0 - current era100% of base100% of baseContemporary research is full price and full time.
1 era behind80% of base80% of baseRecently old research is easier to recover.
2 eras behind60% of base60% of baseMid-game catch-up becomes meaningful.
3 eras behind40% of base40% of baseOld research is cheap and quick, but not free.
4 or more eras behind30% of base30% of baseMaximum depreciation; the 30% floor applies.

effective cost = base cost x depreciation multiplier

effective research time = base time x depreciation multiplier

Values should be rounded using one shared rule before resources are deducted or a queue is created. The technology definition in the static database remains unchanged; depreciation is a runtime resolution layer based on the current world era.

Example: two eras behind

Technology base cost: 100 wood + 50 gold
          
The player can recover selected older research without making the research completely free.

Example: maximum difference

Technology base cost: 200 wood + 100 gold
          
The strongest catch-up still consumes resources and queue time. It cannot drop to zero.
Important distinction: depreciation applies only to technologies that belong to the selected history. A technology from the omitted branch is not an old technology at 30%; it is unavailable at 0% availability.

8. Entity Levels versus Technologies

The system should keep two progression layers separate so that global history does not remove the predictable basic improvement ladder.

LayerScopeBranch dependenceExample
Unit/building levelOne database unit or building definition.Shared and stable across branch choices.Infantry level 2 gains HP, armor, and attack from its next level record.
Functional technologyPlayer-wide filters, production rules, economy, or capabilities.Usually belongs to a selected era branch.Farms gather more efficiently or military buildings train faster.
Historical doctrinePlayer-wide strategic identity or a global branch effect.May be locked by the world's selected history.A military path improves mobilization while imposing an economic trade-off.

The world era controls what can be researched and how much it costs; it does not rewrite every unit level record.

9. UI and Information Visibility

The top-of-screen era bar should communicate the direction of the world without exposing exact enemy state through the fog of war.

UI elementRecommended information
Current eraEra name, number, and time remaining before the passive safety valve.
Global progressNormalized progress for the active condition set.
Player contributionThe local player's share or normalized contribution to each visible condition.
Branch splitProgress toward each candidate branch, with the leading direction visually emphasized.
Enemy informationAggregate direction or a coarse progress range, not exact hidden unit and building counts.
Technology lock reasonSelected branch, omitted branch, missing prerequisite, missing building, insufficient resources, or already researched.

The player sees actionable direction and personal contribution, while hidden enemy details remain aggregated.

Exact wording and whether the player sees a percentage, range, or qualitative label are UI balance decisions. The underlying global controller still uses precise simulation values.

10. Runtime and Save/Load Implications

Era progression is global simulation state, not a UI-only calculation. It must be updated at a deterministic simulation phase and persisted in scene saves.

10.1 World state

WorldEraState

  eraStartedTick
  livingCivilizationCount

  branchProgress[]
  selectedBranch[]
  branchResolutionState[]
  passiveTransitionLimit

The exact field names are illustrative. The important boundary is that the state belongs to the world, while completed technologies belong to individual players.

10.2 Deterministic update point

The controller should read updated player possession and defeat state after the simulation has applied the changes relevant to that tick. It should publish a transition command rather than mutating every player's technology state during a collection iteration.

  • Use fixed simulation ticks for the timer.
  • Evaluate defeat status before calculating the next living-player average.
  • Read completed buildings and units from authoritative possession state.
  • Resolve a branch only once and keep the decision stable after it is committed.
  • Apply the new era before the next technology availability and depreciation query.

10.3 Save/load

Save files need both the global era state and player-specific research state. A save taken during a transition must restore the era timer, branch progress, selected branches, defeated players, completed technologies, and any active research queue consistently.

11. Balance Risks and Open Decisions

The core rules are decided. The following items are intentionally left as balance or content decisions rather than hidden assumptions.

Active progress falling

Decide whether losing workers or buildings reduces the current progress bar, or whether progress records the best achieved value. A live-value model makes raids meaningful; a best-value model prevents progress from oscillating.

Branch threshold format

Decide whether a branch resolves immediately at a threshold or must remain above it for a short fixed duration. A hold duration prevents one temporary production spike from deciding history.

Timeout tie-breaker

Choose the deterministic fallback when branch progress is tied at the passive timer limit: neutral outcome, map data, or a stable branch priority.

Elimination timing

The living-only rule is fixed, but the exact tick at which defeat is committed must match the game's authoritative defeat flow.

Technology rounding

Choose one rounding policy for multiplied integer costs and queue durations. The same policy must be used by UI previews, resource deduction, and queue completion.

Branch power budget

Each selected branch should have a distinct identity, but its exclusive technologies must not make the omitted branch an automatic losing outcome on every map.

Primary risk: because elimination removes a divisor, a winning player may accelerate the next era by defeating a weak opponent. This is acceptable if it is visible and balanced, but it should be tested deliberately in scenarios where one player is close to defeat and the world is close to a threshold.

12. Recommended First Vertical Slice

Build one complete transition before adding the full tree: Era 1 to Era 2 with one ordinary condition, two branch candidates, living-player averaging, a passive timeout, branch locking, and one technology cost preview.
1

Global era state

Add a world-owned current era, era start tick, living-player filter, and deterministic update function. Test the average with two, three, and four living civilizations.

2

Branch progress

Add two data-defined candidate condition sets. Resolve the first completed candidate and permanently lock the omitted branch for the match.

3

Passive timeout

Advance the world when the active condition is not completed before the fixed maximum duration. Test the timeout tie-breaker deterministically.

4

Technology depreciation

Implement the 100/80/60/40/30 schedule for both resource cost and research time. Verify that omitted-branch technologies remain unavailable even when old enough for the 30% floor.

5

UI and save/load

Display current era, timer, local contribution, and branch direction. Save and restore the world state around a transition and around a player elimination.

  • Unit and building level progression remains available on every path.
  • Research cost and time previews match the queue values exactly.
  • Eliminated civilizations disappear from the average at the intended simulation tick.
  • Selected branch state survives save/load and cannot be reset by loading player technology rows.
  • The UI never presents an omitted-branch technology as merely expensive or outdated; it shows it as unavailable.
